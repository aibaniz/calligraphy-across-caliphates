In Arabic, the word khatt — often translated as calligraphy — carries layered meanings: it refers not only to writing and script, but also to lines, traces, and paths. Rooted in pre-Islamic poetry, where khatt evoked the fading marks of deserted campsites, the term embodies both aesthetic form and a deep sense of historical memory. This dual meaning — visual beauty and cultural trace — continues to resonate in Arabic calligraphy today.
